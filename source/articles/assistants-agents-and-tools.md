# Templates, assistants, agents and choosing tools

**Covers:** Prompt template, AI assistant, AI agent, Free tier, Image generator

## From one-off chats to repeatable help {#overview}
Once you have a few prompts that work, the next step is to stop rewriting them. Then comes a choice of tools: some are built into the apps you already use, some act more independently, and plans range from free to expensive. This article explains the building blocks and how to choose among them.

## Prompt template {#prompt-template}
A prompt template is a reusable prompt with blanks that you fill in each time, so that you do not have to rewrite the same instructions. A template for a meeting summary might read: "Summarize the meeting notes below for [audience]. List decisions, actions with owners and dates, and open questions. Keep it under [number] words. Notes: [paste]."

The blanks, shown here in square brackets, hold what changes. The rest holds what you worked out once: the structure, the constraints and the tone. A good template saves time and gives consistent results across a team.

## AI assistant {#ai-assistant}
An AI assistant is an AI helper built into an app you already use, such as your email, documents or spreadsheet, which can draft, summarize or answer questions about your content. The advantage is convenience: it sees the document you have open, so you do not copy and paste. The caution is the same as for any AI: check the result, and know what data the feature sends out.

## AI agent {#ai-agent}
An AI agent is an AI system that can plan and carry out several steps on its own to reach a goal, such as searching the web, writing a report and sending it. Where a chatbot answers one message at a time, an agent decides what to do next, uses tools and continues until it thinks it has finished.

That autonomy is powerful and risky. Mistakes can build up over many steps, and an agent with access to your email or files can do real damage. Give it the least access that does the job, review the plan before it acts, and keep a person approving any step that sends, pays or deletes.

## Free tier {#free-tier}
A free tier is the no-cost version of an online service, usually limited in how much you can use it, which models or features you get and sometimes how your data is handled. It is good for trying a tool and for light use. Check the limits and the privacy terms. Free consumer plans often use your chats to improve the product, and paid business plans often do not.

## Image generator {#image-generator}
An image generator is an AI tool that creates pictures from a written description. The same rules apply as for text: be specific about the subject, the style, the setting and the shape of the picture. Check the output carefully for errors, such as extra fingers or garbled text, and check the licence terms before commercial use. Some tools restrict commercial use on free plans.

## A worked example {#example}
A small team writes weekly status updates and wants to use AI.

| Step | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A prompt template with blanks for audience, length and the notes | The same structure every week |
| 2 | An AI assistant in the team's document app | Sees the notes already open, so there is no copying |
| 3 | An agent to collect updates from the chat and draft the report? Not yet | It would read private messages and send email, so the team waits until there are clear limits |
| 4 | A free tier to test for a month | Low risk; checks whether it helps |
| 5 | Check the privacy terms: the free tier trains on chats, so no client names go in | Protects confidential data |
| 6 | After a month, move to a business plan if it saves time | Better privacy and higher limits |

Count the benefit: if the update took 45 minutes a week by hand and now takes 15, the team saves 30 minutes a week, which is 26 hours a year for each person.

Matching tool to task works the same way. Use a general chatbot for drafting and thinking. Use the assistant inside an app for work on a specific document. Use an image generator for illustrations. Save agents for well-bounded jobs where a mistake is easy to undo.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Choosing the most powerful tool for every job. An agent is more than you need for a one-paragraph email. Another mistake is staying on a free plan for sensitive work without reading what it does with your data.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A prompt template turns a good prompt into a reusable form with blanks.
- An AI assistant works inside an app. An AI agent plans and acts across several steps, so limit its access.
- A free tier is good for trials. Check its limits and its data terms.
- Match the tool to the task and keep a person in charge of risky steps.
