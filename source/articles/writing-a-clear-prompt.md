# Writing a clear prompt

**Covers:** Prompt, Prompting, Persona, Tone, Output format, Constraint

## You get what you ask for {#overview}
An AI tool answers the request it receives, not the one in your head. A vague request produces a generic answer, and a specific one produces something you can use. Writing a good request is a learnable skill, not a talent, and it pays off every time you use the tool. In this article we will take a good request apart and look at each of its parts.

## Prompt {#prompt}
Whatever you type to an AI is a prompt, and everything the tool does next is based on it. A useful prompt has four parts:

1. **Task:** what you want done ("write", "summarize", "compare").
2. **Context:** the background the AI cannot know ("this is for new customers who have never used the product").
3. **Details:** the specifics that decide the result: the audience, the length, the key facts.
4. **Format:** what the answer should look like.

The more complete the prompt, the less the AI must guess.

## Prompting {#prompting}
Prompting is the skill of writing prompts so that the result is what you wanted. It is closer to briefing a capable colleague than to using a search box. Give the AI the same information you would give a new assistant on their first day, because it knows nothing about your situation.

## Persona {#persona}
You can ask the AI to take a role, such as a friendly editor, a careful accountant or a patient tutor. That role is a persona. It sets the style, the vocabulary and the level of caution. "Act as an experienced editor and tighten this paragraph" gives a different result from the bare request.

A persona shapes how the answer sounds. It does not give the AI skills or facts that it lacks, so do not assume that an "expert" persona makes the content more accurate.

## Tone {#tone}
Tone is the attitude of a piece of writing, such as formal, friendly, firm, warm or neutral. Name the tone you want, and give a clue if you can: "friendly but professional, like an email to a colleague I know well". Without that, the AI picks a default that tends to sound generic and over-enthusiastic.

## Output format {#output-format}
Next, say what shape you want the answer in. That shape is the output format: a table, a list of bullet points, numbered steps, an email, three options or a short paragraph. Asking for the format saves editing time and makes the answer easier to scan. If you plan to paste the result into something else, say so: "a table with columns for task, owner and date".

## Constraint {#constraint}
A constraint is a rule that limits the answer: a word limit, a list of things to avoid, a reading level, a required term. Constraints focus the answer and prevent common problems, such as too much length or jargon. State them as clear rules: "no more than 120 words", "avoid technical terms", "do not mention pricing".

## A worked example {#example}
You need a message to customers about a delayed delivery. Let's compare two prompts.

**Weak prompt:** "Write an email about a delay."

**Strong prompt:**

| Part | What you write |
|---|---|
| Persona | You are a customer service writer for a small online shop |
| Task | Write an email telling customers their order will arrive late |
| Context | The delay is 3 days because of a supplier problem. We will give a 10 percent discount code |
| Details | The reader is a regular customer who is not technical |
| Tone | Apologetic, warm and honest, not defensive |
| Output format | An email with a subject line, then three short paragraphs |
| Constraints | Under 120 words. No jargon. Do not blame the supplier by name |

The weak prompt has about 6 words and leaves the AI to invent the delay, the length and the tone. The strong prompt has about 90 words and fixes all of them. The extra words take a minute to write and often save two or three rounds of corrections.

Check the result against your constraints: count the words, check there is a subject line and make sure the discount appears. You, not the AI, are responsible for the final message.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is writing a very short prompt and then blaming the tool. The opposite is also a slip: a long, tangled prompt with conflicting rules. Keep each part clear and ordered, and put the most important instruction first.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A good prompt states the task, the context, the details and the format.
- A persona and a tone shape the style of the answer, and do not add real expertise.
- Ask for an output format so that the answer is easy to use.
- Constraints such as a length limit focus the result.
