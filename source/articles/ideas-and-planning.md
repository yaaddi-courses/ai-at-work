# Brainstorming and testing your own thinking

**Covers:** Brainstorming, Devil's advocate

## A partner that never gets tired {#overview}
Two of the best uses of AI have nothing to do with getting a single right answer. One is generating many options, so that you do not settle on the first idea. The other is the reverse: finding the holes in a plan you have already made. A tool that is available at any hour and never takes offence suits both jobs.

## Brainstorming {#brainstorming}
When you need ideas, the trick is to produce many quickly without judging them first, and to choose the best ones afterwards. That is brainstorming. The advertising executive Alex Osborn popularized the method in the 1940s. His main rule was to defer judgment: keep the creation of ideas separate from their evaluation, because criticism too early stops ideas before they appear.

AI is good at the first stage because it can produce twenty options in seconds and has no ego. Here are some useful ways to ask:
- Ask for quantity: "Give me 20 ideas", not "give me a good idea".
- Ask for variety: "Include some safe ideas, some unusual ones and some from other industries."
- Give limits that matter: your budget, your audience, what you have tried.
- Ask for a second round: "Combine ideas 3 and 11" or "make idea 7 cheaper".

Then you do the judging. Pick, discard and refine with your own knowledge of the situation, which the AI lacks.

## Devil's advocate {#devils-advocate}
After you have a plan, you need someone to attack it. A devil's advocate argues against an idea on purpose to expose its weak points. The phrase comes from the Catholic Church, which once appointed an official to argue against candidates for sainthood.

You can ask an AI to play the role: "Here is my plan. Act as a skeptical manager and give me the five strongest objections." It helps you meet objections before your audience does. It also counters the AI's tendency to agree with you, as long as you ask it to disagree explicitly.

Ask for specifics: "What would have to be true for this to fail? What are the costs I have not counted? Who would object, and why?" Then judge each objection yourself, because some will be strong and some will be weak.

## A worked example {#example}
You plan a monthly lunch event to build team spirit. Let's use both tools.

**Brainstorm.** Prompt: "Give me 15 ideas for a team event for 12 people, with a budget of 300 dollars a month. Mix safe and unusual ideas." You receive 15 options. You keep four: a shared lunch, a trivia afternoon, a walking meeting and a skills swap.

**Devil's advocate.** Prompt: "I plan to hold a monthly shared lunch for 12 people at 25 dollars each. Act as a skeptical finance manager and give me the five strongest objections."

| Objection | Your judgment |
|---|---|
| 12 x 25 = 300 dollars a month, which uses the whole budget | Valid, so you check for cheaper options |
| Not everyone eats the same food | Valid, so you add dietary choices |
| Remote staff are left out | Valid, so you plan a call-in version |
| People may not want to give up a lunch break | Partly valid, so you make it optional |
| It may feel like forced fun | Weak, because you ask for feedback after the first two events |

Notice the arithmetic: the objection about cost is right, because 12 people times 25 dollars is exactly 300. Without it, you would have spent the entire budget on one item.

You end up with a better plan and a list of replies to prepare. The two steps took about 15 minutes.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is judging ideas while they are being generated, or accepting the first five. Set a number and wait.

A second slip is treating the AI's objections as a verdict. They are prompts for your own thinking, and some of them are wrong.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Brainstorming works best when you separate making ideas from judging them.
- Ask the AI for many options, with variety and your own limits.
- A devil's advocate finds the weak points in a plan. Ask the AI to disagree explicitly.
- You make the final judgment, since the AI does not know your situation.
